About

Cosmology asks where the Universe came from, what it is made of and where it is going.

I work on these questions as a theoretical and computational cosmologist, in a field where physics and mathematics are closely woven together. I am a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Theoretical Physics at the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research (TIFR) in Mumbai.

My research focuses on the large-scale structure of the Universe: the vast cosmic web of galaxies and gas. I study the higher-order correlations in this structure through galaxy clustering and fluctuations in the 21-cm signal from neutral hydrogen (HI). I also work on constraining cosmological and galaxy-formation parameters, and on building simulations for cosmological studies.

I did my PhD at IIT Kharagpur under the supervision of Prof. Somnath Bharadwaj. There I studied the bispectrum, a three-point statistic, of galaxy and HI distributions, using both observational data and simulations. I am a member of the SKA-India consortium on 21-cm cosmology, which covers Cosmic Dawn, the Epoch of Reionization and the post-reionization era.

Now

What I am working on

Late-time Universe

Galaxies as a 3D map

Galaxy surveys give us an information-rich, three-dimensional map of the Universe. I build tools that extract as much of that information as possible, including on small, non-linear scales, to understand how the Universe has evolved at late times.

Epoch of Reionization

Hydrogen and the first light

The 21-cm line of neutral hydrogen lets us study the Epoch of Reionization. We map the sky at radio frequencies with interferometers and extract the information carried by 21-cm fluctuations. I am currently using 21-cm forest data to constrain models of reionization.
